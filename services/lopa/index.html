What is LOPA?

Layer of protection analysis (LOPA) is a semi-quantitative risk assessment method used to decide whether the safeguards against a specific hazardous scenario are sufficient. It usually takes scenarios from a HAZOP, estimates how often the initiating event occurs, credits each independent protection layer (IPL) with a probability of failure on demand, and compares the resulting frequency with the organisation’s tolerable risk criteria. Where a gap remains, LOPA quantifies the additional risk reduction needed, often as a safety integrity level (SIL) for a safety instrumented function.

Why it matters

  • It concentrates effort on the scenarios that drive risk instead of adding protection everywhere.
  • It tests whether claimed safeguards are genuinely independent, effective and auditable.
  • It provides the SIL targets that IEC 61511 requires for safety instrumented systems in the process industry.
  • It leaves a documented, defensible basis for risk decisions that can be reviewed by regulators, insurers and future teams.

When it is needed

  • After a HAZOP has identified high-consequence scenarios
  • When a safety instrumented system is designed, extended or modified
  • When SIL targets need revalidation after process or operating changes
  • When an existing plant’s SIL assignments lack documented justification

Method

  1. Select scenarios from the HAZOP, normally those with severe consequences.
  2. Define each consequence and its tolerable frequency from the company risk criteria.
  3. Identify the initiating events and their frequencies, using company data or recognised sources such as the CCPS guidelines.
  4. Test each candidate IPL against the criteria of independence, effectiveness and auditability, and assign a probability of failure on demand.
  5. Apply conditional modifiers, such as probability of ignition or occupancy, only where they are justified.
  6. Calculate the mitigated frequency, identify any gap and determine the required SIL or other risk reduction.
  7. Record assumptions, open issues and recommendations.

SIL targets

For safety instrumented functions in low-demand mode, IEC 61508 and IEC 61511 define the following bands:

SIL Average probability of failure on demand Risk reduction factor
SIL 1 0.01 to 0.1 10 to 100
SIL 2 0.001 to 0.01 100 to 1,000
SIL 3 0.0001 to 0.001 1,000 to 10,000

A SIL 3 requirement, or a target that cannot be met by a single function, is usually a signal to revisit the design or add other layers of protection rather than to rely on instrumentation alone.

Functional safety support

Beyond the LOPA workshop, specialists can help with SIL verification (checking that the designed function meets its target, including architecture, failure data and proof-test intervals), safety requirements specification input, proof-test procedure reviews and preparation for functional safety assessments. IEC 61511 also requires a security risk assessment of the safety instrumented system, which links to OT cybersecurity.

Information required

  • HAZOP report and worksheets for the scenarios in scope
  • P&IDs, cause and effect diagrams and SIS documentation
  • The company risk criteria and any existing LOPA procedure
  • Initiating event and IPL data used by the organisation
  • For existing plants: trip, demand and test history

Deliverables

  • LOPA worksheets for every scenario assessed
  • IPL register with the justification for each credit
  • SIL target list for safety instrumented functions
  • Recommendations for additional or alternative protection
  • Report with assumptions and data sources; optional SIL verification calculations

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between HAZOP and LOPA?

HAZOP identifies what can go wrong and lists the safeguards. LOPA takes selected high-consequence scenarios and tests, semi-quantitatively, whether those safeguards reduce the risk to a tolerable level.

What makes a safeguard an independent protection layer?

It must be independent of the initiating event and of other layers credited, effective at preventing the specific consequence, and auditable through testing or inspection. Alarms, relief devices and safety instrumented functions can qualify if they meet these criteria.

Does IEC 61511 require LOPA?

IEC 61511 requires a hazard and risk assessment and the allocation of safety functions to protection layers, with a SIL for each safety instrumented function. It does not mandate LOPA specifically, but LOPA is one of the most widely used methods.

Should LOPA be held in the same workshop as HAZOP?

Usually not. A separate, shorter LOPA session after the HAZOP, with a smaller team that includes process, safety and instrumentation specialists, gives better results.
