What an owner’s engineer does

An owner’s engineer (OE) is an engineering team, or a single senior engineer, that works for the asset owner rather than the contractor. The contractor is paid to deliver to the contract; the owner’s engineer makes sure that what is delivered is what the owner actually needs, at the quality specified, and that problems are found while they are still cheap to fix. Owners without large in-house engineering departments, such as developers, investors and public authorities, rely on an OE to act as their technical function.

An independent technical advisor (ITA), sometimes called a lender’s technical advisor or independent engineer, plays a similar role for lenders and investors: an objective view of technical and schedule risk, and verification of progress before funds are released.

The owner’s engineer across the project lifecycle

Stage Typical owner’s engineer activities
Concept Option assessment, basis of design, technology review, owner’s requirements
FEED Contracting strategy input, FEED supervision and independent design review, estimate and schedule review
EPC Tender documents, technical bid evaluation, contract technical schedules, design review and approval workflow
Construction Quality surveillance, progress verification, change and claims review, construction assurance
Commissioning Witnessing tests, completions verification, commissioning and start-up oversight
Handover As-built and data handover checks, performance test witnessing, punch-list close-out, defects period support

Concept, FEED, EPC, construction, commissioning and handover each carry different risks; the owner’s engineer team should change shape as the project moves through them.

Why owners use an OE

  • Contractors optimise for their contract; owners must live with the asset for decades
  • Design decisions made without operations input are expensive to reverse
  • Progress reports are only as good as the verification behind them
  • Change orders and claims need technical as well as commercial assessment
  • Handover data and documentation are often the last priority for a contractor near completion

How AATTRL provides owner’s engineering

AATTRL assembles OE teams from specialists who have worked on both sides: EPC contractors and operating companies. Teams can be a single senior advisor attending key reviews, a part-time group covering defined disciplines, or a fuller embedded team. Scope is agreed by stage and can grow or shrink as the project moves forward.

Information required

The contract or draft contract, scope of work and specifications, the project schedule and current status, the owner’s standards and requirements, and any reports or concerns that have prompted the request.

Deliverables

  • Design review comment registers and approval recommendations
  • Monthly or milestone monitoring reports for owners, boards or lenders
  • Change order and claims technical assessments
  • Quality surveillance and witness reports
  • Input to payment, completion and acceptance certificates
  • Handover and close-out reports

Owners and investors often combine owner’s engineering with technical due diligence at the investment stage. To understand the full lifecycle, see From FEED to Operations.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an owner's engineer and a PMC?

A project management consultant (PMC) usually manages the project on the owner’s behalf, including contractors and controls. An owner’s engineer focuses on technical oversight. On some projects the two roles are combined.

When should an owner's engineer be appointed?

Ideally at concept or early FEED, so that owner requirements shape the design and the contract. An OE appointed during construction can still add value, but has less influence.

Can a single specialist act as owner's engineer?

For smaller projects, yes. A senior engineer can cover technical oversight, supported by discipline specialists for specific reviews.

Do you act as lender's independent engineer?

AATTRL can provide independent technical advisors for lenders and investors, subject to confirming independence from the project parties and the qualifications the lender requires.
