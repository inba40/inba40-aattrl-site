Is your maintenance programme doing the right work on the right equipment? AATTRL provides maintenance and RCM specialists who build strategies based on how equipment actually fails, so effort goes where it reduces risk and cost.

Why maintenance programmes drift

Most maintenance programmes start from vendor recommendations and grow by addition: a task added after each failure, an inspection added after each audit. Over time they contain work that adds little value, miss failure modes that matter, and leave planners unable to complete the schedule. Maintenance optimization resets the programme on a clear basis: what each asset must do, how it can fail, the consequence of failure, and the most effective way to manage it.

Approaches

Equipment criticality assessment

Ranks assets by the consequences of failure for safety, environment, production and cost. Criticality sets the depth of analysis and the priority of work.

Reliability-centred maintenance (RCM)

A rigorous analysis of functions, failure modes and consequences for critical systems. SAE JA1011 sets out the criteria a process must meet to be called RCM.

PM optimization

A faster review of existing preventive maintenance tasks for less critical equipment: removing low-value tasks, correcting frequencies and adding missing failure modes.

FMECA

Failure mode, effects and criticality analysis of equipment or systems, often used to build maintenance strategies for new plants before operating history exists.

Task selection

For each relevant failure mode, the analysis chooses the most effective task: condition-based monitoring where failures give warning, time-based restoration or replacement where wear is predictable, failure-finding tests for hidden failures of protective devices, redesign where no task is effective, or run-to-failure where the consequence is acceptable. The result is a programme with a reason for every task.

Spares, shutdowns and work management

  • Spares optimization: linking spare holdings to criticality and lead time, and identifying capital spares for long-lead critical equipment.
  • Shutdown and turnaround readiness: scope challenge and freeze, work-pack quality, materials readiness and readiness reviews before the event.
  • Work management: planning and scheduling practices, backlog management and the measures that show whether the programme is being executed.

Information required

  • Asset register and hierarchy
  • Current maintenance plans and task lists
  • Maintenance history and failure data
  • Operating context: duty, redundancy, production impact
  • Vendor manuals for equipment without history

Deliverables

  • Equipment criticality register
  • Maintenance strategies by equipment type or system
  • Optimised task lists ready to load into the CMMS
  • Spares recommendations and capital spares list
  • Turnaround readiness assessment, where in scope
  • Summary of expected changes in workload, cost and risk

Maintenance optimization follows naturally from reliability analysis and feeds the asset management system. For new plants it is part of operational readiness. For the difference between the two main approaches, read RCM vs Preventive Maintenance.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between RCM and PM optimization?

RCM starts from the functions and failure modes of a system and builds the maintenance programme from first principles. PM optimization starts from the existing tasks and improves them. RCM suits critical systems; PM optimization is faster for the rest.

Do we need RCM for every asset?

No. A criticality assessment first identifies where full RCM is justified. Lower-criticality equipment usually gets a simpler, template-based strategy.

Can you load the results into SAP or Maximo?

Yes. Specialists can prepare task lists and maintenance plans in a format ready for loading into SAP, IBM Maximo or other CMMS platforms, and help verify the loaded data.

How long does a maintenance optimization project take?

A criticality assessment and PM optimization for a single plant typically takes a few weeks to a few months, depending on the number of assets and data quality.
