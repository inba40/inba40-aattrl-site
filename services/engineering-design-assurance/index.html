Engineering Design Reviews and Design Assurance

Does a project require an independent FEED review before a final investment decision, or a second opinion on a contractor’s detailed design? AATTRL provides senior, multidisciplinary reviewers who check the design against its purpose, the standards and the way the plant will be built, operated and maintained.

What design assurance is

Design assurance gives the owner confidence that the design will deliver what the project needs: safely, at the expected cost and schedule, and in a form that can be built, operated and maintained. It is not a repeat of the contractor’s own checking. It is an independent view, by people who have seen designs succeed and fail, of whether the design is complete, consistent and fit for purpose.

Reviews by project stage

Stage Typical review focus
Feasibility Technical viability, key risks, technology choice, order-of-magnitude cost and schedule
Concept Option comparison, basis of design, layout concept, safety and environmental screening
FEED Completeness and maturity for the EPC contract, design basis, process and equipment selection, safety studies, cost estimate maturity
Detailed design Discipline and interdisciplinary checks, model reviews, vendor data integration, compliance with codes and specifications
Procurement Technical bid evaluation, vendor engineering, deviations and clarifications

Cost estimate maturity is commonly expressed with recognised classifications, such as those of AACE International, which help owners judge whether an estimate is fit for the decision being made.

Specialist review types

Constructability reviews

Sequencing, access, lifting, modularisation, site constraints and the effect of design choices on construction risk.

Operability reviews

Start-up, shutdown and abnormal operation, control philosophy, alarm load, sampling, isolation and the operator’s view of the plant.

Maintainability reviews

Access for inspection and maintenance, lifting and removal routes, isolation for maintenance, standardisation and spares.

Value engineering

Structured workshops that reduce cost or schedule without losing required function, with each idea assessed for its effect on risk and lifecycle cost.

Multidisciplinary reviews

Many design problems sit between disciplines: a process change not reflected in the piping, an electrical load list that has not caught up with equipment changes, an instrument that cannot be reached. Multidisciplinary reviews bring process, mechanical, piping, electrical, instrumentation, civil and structural reviewers together to check consistency across the design.

Vendor engineering and technical bid evaluation

Specialists review technical bids against requisitions, identify and assess deviations, prepare clarification questions and recommend technically acceptable bidders. After award, they review vendor documents and manage technical queries so that package data reaches the design on time.

Information required

The design basis and the documents for the stage under review: process flow diagrams and P&IDs, layouts, equipment and line lists, specifications, studies, cost estimate and schedule, and the owner’s standards.

Deliverables

  • Review comment register with priorities and responses tracked to closure
  • Findings report with conclusions on design maturity and key risks
  • Recommendations for decision gates, such as readiness for an EPC tender or final investment decision
  • Technical bid evaluation reports

Independent reviews are a core part of an owner’s engineer role and of technical due diligence for investors. Hazard studies such as HAZOP run alongside design reviews. For what to prepare, see From FEED to Operations.

Frequently asked questions

What is a FEED review?

An independent review of the front-end engineering design package to confirm it is complete, consistent and mature enough for the next decision, typically an EPC tender or final investment decision.

How long does an independent design review take?

A focused review of one discipline can take a few days. A full multidisciplinary FEED review usually takes two to six weeks depending on scope and document volume.

Can you review a single discipline?

Yes. Reviews can be limited to a discipline, a package or a specific concern, such as rotating equipment selection or electrical system design.

Do AATTRL reviewers approve or stamp designs?

No. Reviewers provide independent technical opinion. Design responsibility, approval and any statutory sign-off remain with the engineer of record and, where required, appropriately licensed professionals in the relevant jurisdiction.
