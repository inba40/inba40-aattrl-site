Pre-Start-Up Safety Review (PSSR) Services

Preparing to introduce hydrocarbons, chemicals or energy into a new or modified facility? AATTRL provides experienced reviewers who confirm, with evidence, that the plant, its safety systems and its people are ready for safe start-up.

What is a PSSR?

A pre-start-up safety review is the final check, before hazardous materials or energy are introduced, that a new or modified facility has been built as designed, that its safety systems work, and that the people, procedures and documents needed to operate it are in place. In the United States, OSHA’s process safety management standard requires a PSSR for new facilities and for modifications significant enough to change process safety information. Most operators worldwide apply the same principle through their own standards.

When it is needed

  • Before the first start-up of a new plant or unit
  • Before restart after a modification managed through MOC
  • Before restart after a turnaround that included changes to the plant
  • For large projects, in stages, system by system or area by area as each becomes ready

What a PSSR checks

Area Typical checks
Design confirmation The built facility matches the approved design; HAZOP and other study recommendations closed or formally deferred with justification
Construction completion Mechanical completion certified; pressure and leak tests complete; temporary blinds, supports and materials removed
Procedures Operating, start-up, shutdown, emergency and maintenance procedures approved and available where they are needed
Training Operators and maintainers trained and assessed on the new or changed equipment
Maintenance readiness Equipment registered in the maintenance system, preventive routines loaded, critical spares available
Safety systems Safety instrumented functions, relief devices, fire and gas detection, fire protection and emergency shutdown tested and in service
Documentation P&IDs and process safety information updated to as-built; relief calculations and cause and effect diagrams current
MOC closure Every related change request reviewed, approved and closed or controlled
Punch-list management Items that prevent safe start-up closed; remaining items assessed, owned and scheduled
Operational acceptance Operations formally accepts care, custody and control from the project or maintenance team

How AATTRL supports PSSR

  • Develops or reviews the PSSR procedure and checklists to suit the facility and the operator’s standards
  • Leads, or joins as an independent member, PSSR teams, including field verification walk-downs
  • Tests the evidence behind “complete” statuses rather than accepting them at face value
  • Classifies findings and tracks them to closure
  • Prepares the start-up recommendation record and captures lessons for the next system
  • Coaches site teams so that future reviews can be run internally

Information required

  • Design and HAZOP action close-out status
  • Mechanical completion certificates, test records and punch lists
  • Operating and maintenance procedures and training records
  • MOC records for the scope
  • Safety instrumented system, fire and gas and emergency shutdown test results
  • The operator’s PSSR standard, if one exists

Deliverables

  • Completed PSSR checklists with references to the evidence reviewed
  • Findings register, split into items to close before start-up and items to close after
  • Start-up recommendation or authorisation record
  • Short lessons-learned note for subsequent systems or projects

PSSR and operational readiness

A PSSR confirms readiness at one point in time; it cannot create readiness that was never planned. Facilities that start operational readiness planning during FEED reach their PSSR with far fewer findings. For the steps leading up to start-up, see commissioning and start-up support and the guide What Should Be Checked Before Plant Start-Up?

Frequently asked questions

Who should lead a PSSR?

A competent person with authority who is independent of the team that built or modified the plant, typically from operations with technical safety support. On major projects an external reviewer adds independence and experience.

Is a PSSR the same as mechanical completion?

No. Mechanical completion confirms that construction is finished and tested. A PSSR goes further, confirming that safety systems, procedures, training, documentation and change management are also ready for safe operation.

Can a facility start up with open punch items?

Yes, if each item has been assessed as not affecting safe start-up and operation, has an owner and a date, and is formally accepted. Items that affect safety, integrity or environmental protection must be closed first.

How long does a PSSR take?

A small modification can be reviewed in a few hours to a day. A new unit usually needs several days per system, spread across the commissioning schedule.
