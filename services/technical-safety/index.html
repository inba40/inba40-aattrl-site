What technical safety covers

Technical safety engineering designs out and controls major accident hazards: fires, explosions and toxic releases. It sets the layout and spacing, the isolation and depressurisation philosophy, fire and gas detection, active and passive fire protection, escape and evacuation, and the assessments that show risk has been reduced as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP). Process safety management is the management system that keeps those protections effective for the life of the facility.

Services

Hazard studies

HAZID, HAZOP, LOPA and SIL, bow-tie analysis and reviews of simultaneous operations.

Risk assessment

Quantitative risk assessment (QRA), fire and explosion risk assessment (FERA), escape, evacuation and rescue assessment (EERA), emergency systems survivability assessment (ESSA) and ALARP demonstrations.

Safety engineering

Safety philosophies, hazardous area classification reviews, fire and gas mapping reviews, active and passive fire protection, firewater systems and emergency shutdown philosophy.

Major accident hazard management

Safety cases, barrier management, safety-critical elements, performance standards and verification schemes.

Process safety management

PSM gap assessments, management of change, pre-start-up safety reviews, process safety indicators and technical safety audits.

Incidents and emergencies

Incident investigation and root cause analysis, emergency response planning and review of drills and exercises.

When clients call us

  • A project needs a technical safety lead, or an owner’s reviewer for contractor safety deliverables
  • A regulator, lender or insurer has asked for a safety case, an ALARP demonstration or a risk study
  • An audit has found weaknesses in barrier management or management of change
  • An incident needs an independent, experienced investigator
  • Process safety indicators are not telling management what it needs to know

Approach

Work starts from the major accident hazards and the decisions the client must make, not from a standard list of studies. Methods are chosen to suit the hazard and the stage, and are explained to the client team. Recommendations are practical and prioritised.

Specialists work to the client’s risk criteria and standards and to recognised references, such as IEC 61511 for functional safety, IEC 60079-10-1 for hazardous area classification, NFPA and EN standards for fire protection, and CCPS and Energy Institute guidance for process safety management. Where national regulation applies, for example COMAH in Great Britain, OSHA process safety management in the United States, or the requirements of regulators and national oil companies in India and the GCC, the scope is aligned with it from the start.

Information required

The starting point depends on the scope: the design basis, layouts, P&IDs and previous studies for projects; the safety management system, incident data and audit history for operating assets.

Deliverables

  • Study reports with assumptions, results and recommendations
  • Safety philosophies and performance standards
  • Safety case sections or complete safety cases for client approval
  • Barrier and safety-critical element registers
  • Audit reports with prioritised findings and practical actions
  • Investigation reports with root causes and corrective actions

Services are delivered by appropriately qualified and experienced specialists according to project, jurisdiction and client requirements.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between technical safety and process safety?

Technical safety is the engineering discipline that designs protection against fires, explosions and toxic releases. Process safety is the wider discipline, including management systems, that keeps hazardous processes under control throughout their life. The two overlap and are often delivered together.

What does ALARP mean?

As low as reasonably practicable: risk has been reduced until the cost, time and effort of further reduction would be grossly disproportionate to the benefit. It is a legal concept in Great Britain and is widely used as a decision principle elsewhere.

Can you prepare a safety case?

Yes. Specialists can prepare or review safety cases and their supporting studies. The safety case remains the duty holder’s document and must reflect how the facility is actually operated.

Do you carry out incident investigations?

Yes. Experienced investigators can lead or support investigations using structured root cause methods, and help turn the findings into corrective actions that address management system causes as well as technical ones.
