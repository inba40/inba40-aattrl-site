From construction to operation

Commissioning turns a constructed facility into a working one. The usual sequence is:

  1. Mechanical completion. Construction is finished, inspected and tested in accordance with the design, and recorded through completion certificates.
  2. Pre-commissioning. Cleaning, flushing, drying, pressure and leak testing, instrument loop checks and electrical tests, system by system.
  3. Commissioning. Equipment and systems are energised and run, first with utilities or inert media, to prove that they work as intended, including control and safety functions.
  4. Start-up. After the pre-start-up safety review, process materials or energy are introduced and the plant is brought to stable operation.
  5. Performance testing and handover. Guaranteed performance is demonstrated, and care, custody and control pass formally to operations.

Where commissioning goes wrong

  • Construction completes in area sequence while commissioning needs complete systems
  • The completions database shows progress that the field does not
  • Vendor representatives are not booked for the dates they are needed
  • Punch lists grow faster than they close
  • Operations staff are not involved until handover, so knowledge is lost
  • Temporary facilities, utilities and chemicals for commissioning are underestimated

Most of these problems are set up during detailed design and early construction, which is why commissioning planning should start well before mechanical completion.

How commissioning connects to the rest of the project

Commissioning depends on design decisions about isolation, drains, vents and test points, on the owner’s engineer keeping the contractor’s completions honest, and on operational readiness delivering trained operators and a working maintenance system. The pre-start-up safety review is the final gate before start-up.

Frequently asked questions

What is systemization?

Dividing the facility into systems and subsystems that can be completed, tested and handed over as units. It aligns construction completion with the order in which commissioning needs systems.

What is the difference between pre-commissioning and commissioning?

Pre-commissioning covers static checks and tests before equipment runs: cleaning, flushing, pressure tests and loop checks. Commissioning energises and runs equipment and systems to prove they work as intended.

Can you provide commissioning staff for a short period?

Yes. Specialists can join for a defined phase, such as FAT witnessing, a single system, or the start-up period.

Do you support data centre commissioning?

Yes. Data centre commissioning follows a levelled approach from factory testing to integrated systems testing of power and cooling. See data centres.
