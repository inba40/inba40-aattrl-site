What asset integrity covers

Asset integrity management keeps equipment that contains hazardous fluids or carries loads in a condition where it can do so safely: pressure vessels, heat exchangers, tanks, piping, pipelines, relief systems and structures. It combines an understanding of how each item degrades with inspection, monitoring, operating limits and timely repair, all supported by records good enough to prove it.

Services

Inspection strategy and risk-based inspection

Inspection plans based on the likelihood and consequence of failure, following recognised practice such as API RP 580 and API RP 581, so inspection effort goes where risk is highest.

Damage mechanism reviews

Identification of credible degradation, such as corrosion, erosion, cracking and creep, for each circuit, drawing on references such as API RP 571.

Integrity operating windows

Limits on process variables that affect degradation, with alarms and responses, in line with API RP 584.

Fitness-for-service

Assessments of flaws and damage found during inspection to decide whether equipment can continue in service, be repaired or be replaced, using API 579-1/ASME FFS-1.

Corrosion management

Corrosion control strategies, chemical treatment and monitoring programmes, and corrosion under insulation reviews.

Ageing assets and life extension

Assessment of remaining life, obsolescence and condition to support decisions on continued operation, upgrade or replacement.

Brownfield and ageing facilities

Many operating facilities are well beyond their original design life, have changed duty several times and have incomplete records. Integrity specialists start by establishing what is known, what is not, and what matters most: a baseline of condition, damage mechanisms and risk, followed by a prioritised plan to close critical gaps. For major hazard facilities, integrity is also linked to safety-critical elements and their performance standards.

Information required

  • Equipment and piping registers, design data and drawings
  • Process conditions and changes in duty over time
  • Inspection history, thickness data and anomaly records
  • Corrosion monitoring and chemical treatment data
  • Previous integrity, RBI or life-extension studies

Deliverables

  • Integrity management strategy and roles
  • Corrosion circuits and damage mechanism review
  • Risk-based inspection plans and schedules
  • Integrity operating window definitions
  • Fitness-for-service and remaining life assessments
  • Anomaly management procedure and register review
  • Life-extension or condition assessment reports

Integrity findings often inform technical due diligence before an acquisition, and they complement reliability work on rotating and electrical equipment. Statutory inspections and certification are carried out by appropriately authorised inspectors where regulations require them.

Services are delivered by appropriately qualified and experienced specialists according to project, jurisdiction and client requirements.

Frequently asked questions

What is risk-based inspection?

A method of planning inspection based on the likelihood and consequence of failure of each item, so that inspection effort and intervals reflect risk rather than fixed calendar periods.

Can you assess an ageing asset for life extension?

Yes. A life-extension assessment reviews condition, degradation, remaining life, obsolescence and safety-critical elements to support a decision on continued operation.

Do you perform statutory inspection?

AATTRL specialists support inspection strategy, planning and assessment. Statutory inspections and certification are performed by inspectors or bodies authorised in the relevant jurisdiction.
