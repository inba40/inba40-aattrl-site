When reliability becomes a problem

Reliability problems rarely announce themselves. They show up as a pump that has had its seal changed six times in two years, a compressor that trips every few weeks, a production plan that never quite delivers, and a maintenance budget that keeps growing while availability does not. The common thread is that the organisation repairs failures efficiently but does not remove their causes.

What a reliability specialist does

A reliability engineer looks at failures as data. Which equipment fails most, how, and why? Which failures cost the most in lost production, safety risk or maintenance effort? What has to change in design, operation or maintenance so they stop? The answers lead to specific actions, not general programmes.

Services

Bad actor identification

Pareto analysis of failures, downtime and maintenance cost to find the small number of assets that cause most of the losses.

Root cause analysis facilitation

Structured RCA of significant or repeat failures, using methods such as 5 Whys, cause and effect analysis and logic trees, chosen to fit the problem.

FMEA and RAM studies

Failure mode and effects analysis of critical systems, and reliability, availability and maintainability modelling to test design and sparing options.

Condition monitoring and predictive maintenance

Design or review of vibration, oil analysis, thermography and ultrasound programmes, and integration of online monitoring and analytics.

Reliability KPIs and data

Meaningful indicators such as mean time between failures and failure rates by equipment class, supported by failure data structured along the lines of ISO 14224.

Defect elimination programmes

Routines that turn individual RCAs into a continuing process, with clear ownership and follow-up.

How an engagement typically runs

  1. Baseline. Review maintenance history, downtime records and production losses; interview operations and maintenance staff.
  2. Prioritise. Identify bad actors and the failure modes with the greatest consequence.
  3. Analyse. Facilitate RCAs on the priority failures and validate causes with evidence.
  4. Act. Agree corrective actions in design, operation and maintenance, with owners and dates.
  5. Sustain. Set up KPIs, review meetings and defect elimination routines; transfer the methods to the site team.

Information required

  • Maintenance work order history from the CMMS, ideally several years
  • Downtime and production loss records
  • Equipment register and criticality ranking, if available
  • Previous RCA reports and condition monitoring results

Deliverables

  • Bad actor list with quantified losses
  • RCA reports with verified causes and corrective actions
  • Reliability improvement plan with priorities, owners and expected benefits
  • Condition monitoring programme review and recommendations
  • KPI definitions and a reporting structure that site teams can maintain

Reliability work often leads to changes in maintenance strategy; see maintenance optimization. For static equipment degradation, see asset integrity. Where good data exists, industrial AI can help detect failures earlier. For background, read RCM vs Preventive Maintenance.

Frequently asked questions

What does a reliability consultant do?

Finds the equipment and failure modes that cause the greatest losses, identifies their root causes and puts practical corrective actions and monitoring in place, so failures stop recurring.

How quickly can a reliability assessment show results?

A bad actor analysis can usually be completed within a few weeks if maintenance data is available. Corrective actions on the top failures often show results within months.

Do you facilitate root cause analysis for a single failure?

Yes. An experienced facilitator can lead an RCA for a significant failure, remotely or on site, and leave the team with the method for future investigations.

Is predictive maintenance always worth it?

No. It pays where failures are costly, show detectable warning signs and give enough time to act. For other equipment, simpler strategies are often better.
