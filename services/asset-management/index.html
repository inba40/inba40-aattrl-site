What asset management means

Asset management is how an organisation realises value from its assets over their whole life: deciding what to acquire, how to operate and maintain it, when to renew it and when to dispose of it, while balancing cost, risk and performance. It is broader than maintenance. It joins up decisions that are often made in separate departments, such as capital planning, engineering, operations, maintenance and finance.

The ISO 55000 series sets out the principles and terminology (ISO 55000), the requirements for an asset management system (ISO 55001) and guidance on applying them (ISO 55002).

Signs an organisation needs asset management support

  • Capital and maintenance budgets are set by last year’s numbers rather than by risk and need
  • Renewal decisions are made in a crisis, after failure
  • Different departments hold different asset registers
  • Leadership cannot see the condition or risk profile of the asset base
  • A regulator, owner or investor is asking for evidence of systematic asset management

Services

Maturity assessment

A structured assessment of asset management capability against ISO 55001 and recognised frameworks such as the GFMAM Asset Management Landscape, with a clear picture of strengths and gaps.

Roadmap and system design

A prioritised improvement roadmap and the core documents: asset management policy, strategic asset management plan (SAMP) and objectives.

Asset management plans

Plans for asset classes or sites covering condition, risk, required interventions and lifecycle costs.

Decision-making frameworks

Risk-based prioritisation of capital and maintenance spending, lifecycle cost analysis and renewal planning.

Asset information

Requirements for asset registers, hierarchies and data quality, linked to digital asset transformation.

ISO 55001 readiness

Gap closure, internal audit preparation and support during certification audits by an accredited certification body.

How an engagement typically runs

  1. Understand objectives. What the organisation needs from its assets and what is driving the work.
  2. Assess maturity. Document review, interviews and workshops across functions.
  3. Prioritise. Agree the gaps that matter most for value and risk.
  4. Build the roadmap. Sequence improvements over 12 to 36 months, with owners and resources.
  5. Implement and coach. Develop the system with the client team so capability stays in-house.

Information required

Organisational objectives, existing policies and plans, asset registers, maintenance and capital planning processes, performance reports and any audit findings.

Deliverables

  • Maturity assessment report and gap analysis
  • Asset management roadmap
  • Policy, SAMP and objectives
  • Asset management plans and decision frameworks
  • ISO 55001 readiness review and internal audit support

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between ISO 55000 and ISO 55001?

ISO 55000 gives the overview, principles and terminology of asset management. ISO 55001 specifies the requirements for an asset management system and is the standard organisations are certified against.

Does AATTRL certify organisations to ISO 55001?

No. Certification is carried out by accredited certification bodies. AATTRL helps organisations assess maturity, build their system and prepare for certification.

How long does an asset management maturity assessment take?

Typically three to six weeks for a single organisation or business unit, depending on size and the number of sites.

Is asset management only for utilities?

No. Any organisation whose performance depends on physical assets, including oil and gas, manufacturing, power, data centres and infrastructure, benefits from systematic asset management.
