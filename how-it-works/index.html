How an engagement runs

Every engagement follows the same spine, from the first confidential conversation to the day the problem is formally handed back, resolved. What varies is the size of the team and how long each stage takes.

  1. Brief

    A confidential conversation with the principal, in person or online. You describe the problem as you see it; we ask the questions a board would not. Within a few days you receive a one-page statement of the problem, what a resolution would look like, who we propose and the terms. If AATTRL is not the right firm, we say so and, where we can, suggest who is.

  2. Diagnose

    A small senior team examines the problem from the inside: the numbers, the people, the decisions already made and the ones being avoided. Interviews are held in confidence. The output is a diagnosis in plain language, reported to you first, including the findings that are uncomfortable.

  3. Decide

    The options are laid out with their consequences, costs and risks made explicit. We give a recommendation and the reasoning behind it. The decision is yours; our job is to make sure it is taken with the full picture and that it will survive contact with the board, the bank or the team.

  4. Deliver

    Where the resolution needs execution, the same senior people stay to make it happen: leading a recovery, running a negotiation, installing an operating cadence, or standing beside the executive who has to carry it. Progress is reported to you at an agreed rhythm, in writing.

  5. Hand back

    The engagement ends when the problem is resolved or the decision has been made and is holding. You receive a closing note: what was found, what was done, what to watch for. Nothing is left running without your agreement.

Terms you can expect

Fixed scope and fee
Each stage is scoped and priced before it starts. A problem that turns out to be larger than briefed is re-scoped with you, not quietly extended.
Confidentiality by default
Engagements are not publicised, interviews are held in confidence, and a non-disclosure agreement is signed before the brief if you prefer.
Independence
The team has no stake in the answer. AATTRL does not sell software, systems or placements as part of an engagement, and declares any connection to a party involved.
Direct access
You deal with the principal and the senior team throughout, by phone, in person and in writing. There is no account manager.

How long it takes

A brief is usually complete within a week of the first conversation. Diagnosis typically takes between two and six weeks depending on the size of the business and the access available. Decisions are reached in a matter of days once the diagnosis is accepted. Delivery runs for as long as the resolution needs, in agreed stages, and is reviewed with you at the end of each.
