HAZID vs HAZOP: What Is the Difference?

HAZID and HAZOP are both hazard identification workshops, and the names are often confused. They answer different questions at different stages of a project. Most process facilities need both.

The difference in one sentence

A HAZID asks “what major hazards does this facility or activity present, and how will we manage them?”, while a HAZOP asks “how could this specific process design deviate from its intent, and are the safeguards adequate?”

Side-by-side comparison

HAZID HAZOP
Purpose Identify major hazards early and define how they will be managed Examine process deviations in detail and test safeguards
Typical timing Concept selection, FEED, before construction, before major changes Late FEED or detailed design; modifications; revalidation
Main inputs Project description, plot plan, process flow diagrams, site data Frozen P&IDs, cause and effect diagrams, process data
Method Hazard categories or guide-word checklist applied to areas or activities Process guide words applied to parameters, node by node
Scope Whole facility and its surroundings, including construction, natural and external hazards The process and its control and safety systems
Team Broad: project, process, safety, operations, construction, environment Focused: facilitator, scribe, process, operations, instrumentation, safety
Duration Usually one to three days Days to weeks, depending on size
Outputs Hazard register, major accident hazards, follow-on study list Detailed worksheets, recommendations, scenarios for LOPA

What a HAZID covers that a HAZOP does not

Because it looks at the facility as a whole, a HAZID covers hazards that never appear on a P&ID: proximity to communities, flooding and seismic risk, transport and logistics, simultaneous operations, security threats, construction activities and environmental sensitivities. These hazards shape layout and spacing, the safety philosophy and the studies needed later, so they must be identified while the concept can still change.

What a HAZOP covers that a HAZID does not

A HAZID cannot examine how a specific control valve failing open affects a downstream vessel, or whether a relief valve is sized for the right case. Those questions need the detail of the P&IDs and the systematic guide-word method of a HAZOP. The HAZOP also finds operability problems, such as start-up difficulties, maintenance isolation issues and nuisance trips, which a HAZID will not reach.

How they work together on a project

  1. Concept HAZID. Compares options and identifies hazards that could rule an option out.
  2. FEED HAZID. Confirms major accident hazards for the selected concept and lists the studies needed in detailed design.
  3. Design HAZOP. Reviews the P&IDs in detail once they are mature.
  4. LOPA and SIL. Tests safeguards for high-consequence HAZOP scenarios.
  5. Construction HAZID. Addresses hazards of building and commissioning the facility.
  6. PSSR. Confirms before start-up that study actions are closed.

Common mistakes

  • Using a HAZID instead of a HAZOP for a process modification, because it is quicker. The detailed deviations that cause most process incidents are missed.
  • Holding the HAZOP too early, before the P&IDs are stable, so it has to be repeated.
  • Holding the HAZID too late, when the layout is fixed and the major decisions it should inform have already been made.
  • Treating the two studies as separate exercises, so HAZID actions are never checked during the HAZOP.

Do operating facilities need HAZIDs?

Yes, in some situations. An operations HAZID is useful when an operating philosophy changes, after an acquisition, when preparing or updating a safety case, or before major brownfield work. For changes to the process itself, a HAZOP of the modified section is usually needed as well.

Choosing facilitators

Both studies benefit from an experienced, independent facilitator and a dedicated scribe. The skills overlap but are not identical: a HAZID facilitator needs a broad view of facility hazards, while a HAZOP facilitator needs deep process knowledge and the discipline to keep a long study consistent.
